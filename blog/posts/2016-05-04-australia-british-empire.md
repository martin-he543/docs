For centuries, perhaps thousands of years, Australia had been inhabited by the native Aboriginal people, after their migration to the continent about 42000 years ago. As sea levels rose, the Aboriginals became isolated from the outside world, so within thousands of years, it had become forgotten. However, following the mass exploration of Europeans, in 1606 the Dutch (specifically Willem Janszoon) landed in Australia and renamed it New Holland. On their arrival they chartered the west and north of Australia, however made no attempt to create a settlement.

Fast forward a century in 1770 James Cook arrived in Australia and mapped its east coast, and named it New South Wales after claiming it for the British. After a humiliating defeat leading to the loss of the American colonies, the British were keen to gain this new ‘land’ and established a settlement called Van Diemen’s Land (i.e. modern day Tasmania.) Throughout the 17th and 18th centuries, Britain was facing major problems with crime. Many of the people convicted of crimes (such as theft) were originally sentenced to travel to Australia. However, with the American Revolution, the convicts had no-where to go, so colonies were set up in Australia to accommodate for them. Life for these convicts was tough away from their families in an unfamiliar environment, and discipline was though. However, some convicts were allowed to apply for grants of land to become settlers. This eventually ended in 1852 due to its cruelty and a shortage in need of labour due to immigration. Many people emigrated hearing the discovery of gold in Australia.

Before the arrival of the British, the Aboriginal population was around 750,000 people. However, by the start of the 20th century it had shrunk by around 90%. Most of these deaths have been attributed with diseases brought by the British. Furthermore, violent clashes often occurred between the British and Aboriginals. This led to widespread resentment and Western farming methods as well as the takeover of their land devastated their way of life. Aboriginals that moved to cities suffered similar problems with discrimination and alcoholism being a particular problem. Fast forward to modern times, and the situation still hasn’t improved. Australia gained partial independence in 1901 and full independence in 1942, however is still part of the Commonwealth and the Crown Realm. Impacts of British rule can still be seen today. Many people in Australia right now are descendants of original settlers; people speak English and Australia continues to have close relations with Britain.
