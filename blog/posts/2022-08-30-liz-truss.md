## Introduction
**Alas, here we go again.** With the likely ascension of a leader with the diplomatic demeanour of a donkey, the picture has hardly seemed bleaker for a nation-once-envied. Now faced with the daunting prospects of **a three-in-one triple whammy of a Winter crisis**- any one of which would be enough to derail a nation - the UK ought to turn to its next leader for hope in these troubled times: a **runaway inflation crisis (Inflation is projected to hit 18.6%, according to the [Financial Times.](https://www.ft.com/content/778e65e1-6ec5-4fd7-98d5-9d701eb29567));** the potential collapse of the nation's chronically-ailed healthcare system; and stratospheric energy bills which could drive millions in poverty and starvation. Economists might also point out the stagnant growth that this country has experienced since the 2008 financial crisis: indeed, the UK is staring down at imminent economic recession.

A sobering thought for a developed nation where millions are already having to choose between having meals and keeping on the power (One in four Britons [skip meals due to inflation, according to Reuters.](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/one-four-britons-skip-meals-due-inflation-survey-shows-2022-05-17/)) as the rising costs start to bite. It is in this troubling, quasi-apocalyptic backdrop that Liz Truss enters the scene. Even for a sensible leader, this situation would be enough to cause misery; coupled with an unstable geopolitical climate and a world tinged with uncertainty (including the possibility of a crisis in the Taiwan Strait as well as the ongoing Russo-Ukranian conflict) Britain has never been in more want of a competent Prime Minister to guide it through choppy waters. And yet, instead of some 21st century Rooseveltian figure, or even a leader with the charisma of Margaret Thatcher, who some so desperately wish to channel *(as opposed to the feeble Theresa May)*, **we have Liz Truss.**

## [Let them eat cake,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_them_eat_cake) or visit the foodbank
Armed with her fanatical-obsession with cheese, including a trade deal with Japan that she secured as Trade Secretary accounting for a **mere hundred-thousand pounds in bi-lateral trade,** she evidently has all that it takes to save us from imminent catastrophe and restore Britain's Imperial might. Or so we are told.

The truth to her status as likely successor to Boris lies not in her own strengths, but rather in the weaknesses of her opponents. Frankly, based on the current state of affairs, she could've just as easily swooned over Conservative members by promising to resolve the cost of living crisis by setting orphanages on fire, and using the heat to generate electricity. To be completely honest, I'm surprised that this hasn't already been added to the 2024 Manifesto.

Her chief opponent, the ever-charming Rishi Sunak, is an uncloseted [tax-evader,](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2022/apr/07/rishi-sunaks-wife-says-its-not-relevant-to-say-where-she-pays-tax-overseas) who admitted on tape to [divert public funds away from deprived areas in favour of wealthier ones,](https://news.sky.com/video/rishi-sunak-says-he-worked-to-divert-public-funds-from-deprived-urban-areas-12666078) made with the parlance of a poorly-made robot, and unsurprisingly, [admitted to having no working-class friends.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p9bbBYcwFOk&ab_channel=TheIndependent) **Also, perhaps most crucially to the Conservative electorate, he's brown.** Having increased the nation's debt-to-GDP ratio to levels unseen since the Second World War, his main line of reasoning which advocates economic prudence is hardly persuasive, especially considering how he later back-tracked upon seeing his chances fade against the more-generous Truss.

Liz Truss' appointment isn't exactly desirable though, either. Her primary economic objective appears to be meeting the 2019 Manifesto by not raising National Insurance, a move which disproportionately benefits members of the upper-middle and upper class. Those earning lower wages could save a mere £60 per annum, or even nothing if they don't pay tax: hardly useful when it comes to an energy bill (The average household's energy bill is projected to hit £3554 by this October, according to [Bloomberg Magazine.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-08-22/uk-energy-bills-for-average-household-projected-to-hit-3-554#:~:text=British%20households%20will%20see%20the,compared%20with%20prices%20in%20April.))  that could be several thousands of pounds by October. Neither candidate has announced any additional economic measures, as of yet, to help vulnerable citizens through the Winter, however, Liz Truss has all but ruled out targetted support for Britons. Experts have already warned that this Winter could be a [healthcare disaster.](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2022/aug/29/horrible-winter-lies-ahead-next-pm-inherit-nhs-knees-crisis)

## Call Me Liz, please

Whether you like him or loathe him, Boris Johnson was always known in the popular conscience as "Boris". Something about his aloof personality made it possible to call a politician on a first-name basis and it not to sound weird, perhaps due to an increased perceived relatability in spite of his [rather posh upbringing.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullingdon_Club)

Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak's attempts to replicate this moniker through their campaigns *"Liz for Leader"* and *"Ready for Rishi"* are grossly misinformed. In the public eye, they will always be considered "Liz Truss" or "Rishi Sunak", if not addressed more formally. Nor do I think most people would like to refer to either of them by their first names after the shit-storm that will be unleashed on them this Winter. A more appropriate moniker would most likely become ```those shitheads in Westminster```.

In any case, the electibility of the Conservatives from this point forth hinges on their ability to meet the unique set of challenges that the UK currently faces, most caused by the government itself. By the looks of it, unless Liz Truss signifies a substantial change of tone from her campaign, lack of financial support may cost the Tories their astounding electoral majority afforded to them by Boris Johnson. They are at least likely to lose the so-called "Red Wall" seats they won over in the North of England in 2019.

## Hated by All
She may not have been able to secure many good trade deals during her time as Trade Secretary, or established good foreign relations as Foreign Secretary. And yet, one thing she has undoubtedly managed to foster is indignation, the world-over. Just last week, she managed to [single-handedly incense the French](https://inews.co.uk/news/world/france-baffled-inexplicable-liz-truss-hostility-president-macron-1819195) after being asked at a TV debate to identify France as a friend or foe, and refusing to answer. It is a very rare achievement to annoy so many people before your tenure has even begun.

It appears not many other nations seem to like her over her hawkish attitude towards just about everything, and her immature persona. It is likely the Biden administration, perhaps one of our greatest allies, is not appreciative over her handling of Northern Ireland. Russia has effectively put her on their hit-list. China is running prime-time segments proclaiming her idiocy: they're not too far wrong. Along every step of the way, Liz Truss has managed to display utter carelessness and outright stupidity in her statements as a Cabinet Minister, and would be very likely to continue this into her premiership. **If a crisis were to break out say, in the Taiwan Strait, or were Russia to use tactical nukes in Ukraine, I wouldn't be surprised at all if she were to help drag the UK into World War III.** At least she has one friend in Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

## Good Luck, Britain.

Well, Britain. You're fucked. In multiple respects. The Brexit fairytale dividends promised in 2016 by Brexiteers haven't materialised, nor have they been much help in the face of the worst inflationary crisis in the G7, as well as a cost-of-living crisis which will have people on the verge of starvation and/or hypothermia. **Looks like we couldn't even take back control of our thermostats.**

**Ultimately, the state of British politics is stuck in a bit of a rut.** If the Conservatives were the party of business and low-taxation, severing free-trade agreements with your largest trading partner wasn't exactly the wisest of moves, and the current rate of tax isn't exactly low. And if you voted expecting fiscal prudence, clearly the past few years have thrown that presumption down the drain. As the main opposition party, Labour at the moment appears weak for the most part, and no longer in touch with the working class voter who is not sure what the party stands for anymore. The SNP are doing what the SNP always do. The Liberal Democrats, post-2015 electoral wipeout, haven't been able to make any tangible gains.

**It seems as though Britain is stuck between a hard place, and an extremely hard place. Rule Britannia.**
