I agree with the statement that materialism and hedonism and immaterialism and asceticism are both sure baths to suffering. This is because of the following premises. First, both hedonism and asceticism are ignorant to certain aspects of the human body. Asceticism is ignorant of the physical form, harming the body, whereas hedonism is oblivious to the mind. Hedonists overly indulge in the world whereas ascetics don’t accept it. Both ways cause suffering. Another criticism of hedonism can be seen in the Paradoxes of Hedonism: it can be said in deliberately seeking pleasures, we often miss them. For example, studying for a test may deny pleasure, but may cause greater pleasures to occur like passing the best and consequently get rewarded by your parents. Because of this, it can be said that hedonism is not the best way to avoid suffering, but a compromise between the two. Siddhartha Gautama avoid suffering in neither hedonism and asceticism, but only in his compromise between the two extremes. Another application of why hedonism (for example) is not the best way to avoid suffering. A man called Robert Nozick asks whether to take an all-positive simulated reality of the real world (representing hedonism.) You have the option to plug into the machine but the catch is you would never be able to plug out. Despite the optimism of the notion of this experiment, there are certainly many different reasons not to plug in. You would never be able to find out the truth, only limited to man-made reality. You would be an ‘indeterminate blob.’

A hedonist might disagree with me and say that ‘life should be lived to the maximum.’ One reason would be to not have any regrets in your life (due to a fear of death) and to enjoy your life. One example would be if tomorrow were to be your death and you had known, you would probably want to make the most out of it. However this view is flawed as the above argument is not valid (non sequitur.) There is no reason to fear death in not knowing what it. Another possible criticism of hedonism comes that if you want to ‘quench’ a pleasure, another craving will occur, which in turn require you to go through suffering to quench (e.g. if you buy the new iPhone, next year you would want it again.) Therefore, the way to get rid of suffering would be to get rid of the desire that makes you want to have the pleasure. Hedonism, on the other hand, is all about satisfying pleasures.

All in all, I can conclude that hedonism and asceticism both lead to suffering. It is only a compromise between asceticism and hedonism, a mix of the two, (the middle way in the case of Siddhartha Gautama,) that can truly rid one of suffering. The body must not be treated too harsh through asceticism, but should not be treated lavishly through hedonism.
