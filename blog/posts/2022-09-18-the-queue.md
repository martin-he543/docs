> “Day 4. I tried to resist the queue. But in the end it was too much. Soon I fear we will all succumb to the queue. The streets will be empty. The entire country will stand as one in The Queue. Only then will we be at peace.”

By noon, The Queue had begun to stretch outside the city, its monster tentacles beginning to penetrate suburbia. And yet, further and further extended The Queue.

People were joining The Queue from all over: Hersham, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Taunton, Abergavenny, Londonderry, Thurso. On and on and on, endless onslaughts of people would keep streaming into the capital, turning the Tube into a stampede-prone bloodbath. And yet, so frenzied and with such resolve were the attempts to join The Queue, that most found themselves completely indifferent to the grotesque melange of trampled individuals that littered the winding catacombs of each station; the mephitic miasma increasingly began to envelope the city, as lifeless eyes looked on, eyes glued to phone screens, awaiting with desperation at every announcement from the Government regarding The Queue.

Looking down the suffocating carriages that hurtled underneath the capital, you could observe an almost-choreographed synchronicity in the movements of the heads of passengers, as their phones pinged in harmony at every news bulletin. Possessed, they anxiously tapped their screens, opening web browsers, revealing the latest position of The Queue. At 5pm, the Tube had to be closed to clear the brutalised corpses that had begun to congest the tracks. From the megaphones, calm, monotomous voices urged the hysteric crowd below to wait their turn, and stop pushing forward from behind. After all, debris on the tracks means everyone at the station would end up further behind The Queue.

Flights were beginning grind to a halt, due to operating at more than 400% maximum capacity. People hid themselves in suitcases, luggage holds, standing in the aisles, in order to claim their spot on their way towards London. Heathrow was near breaking point as people overwhelmed security, taking what they needed from Duty Free shopping, with callous regard for the "law" and "order". Nothing matter now. All that mattered now was The Queue.
