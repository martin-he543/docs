In the years leading to 1688, the reign of James II had become more and more turbulent, with increasingly Catholic views. 1689 was the year that saw the ‘Glorious Revolution,’ the overthrowing of the controversial monarch as well as the invitation of a foreign noble to replace him jointly with James’ own daughter to replace him, and a wave of changes to the way England was ruled. I believe that these waves of changes were a turning point in a way the relationship between the King and Parliament.

1689 was a year of change for the relationship between the King and Parliament. The Bill of Rights passed in 1689 denied William III to raise taxes without Parliament’s permission and also let Parliament decide how much money they would grant the King. These changes were needed to help control the king’s spending, probably an attempt to avoid levels of spending achieved during Charles II reign, and the other change to avert situations such as with Charles I’s Ship Money in 1634. Another change was that MPs had the right to free speech in the Hoses of Commons in order to debate matters. Before 1689, nearly every Stuart monarch had neglected Parliament. Dissolving it (James I, Charles I, Cromwell, Charles II, James II), replacing it entirely (Cromwell’s Protectorate Parliament in 1654, James II) and in some cases storming Parliament (Charles II.) Thus, 1689 was a turning point in the Parliament-King relationship.

However, many attitudes from previous years continued into William III’s reign. Although William III could not raise taxes without permission or get the amount of money he asked for, he bypassed it by setting up the Bank of England that allowed him to give himself money. And although William III didn’t storm Parliament like Charles I, he didn’t necessarily respect their elections and had the right to call them. Because of this, much stayed the same.

In conclusion, overall 1689 was a turning point in Parliament getting more power. It allowed Parliament to have control, and even though the king found ways to avoid it, it still meant change in turning England from virtually a dictatorship to a constitutional monarchy. These changes as it saw transition of England to a more democratic country, if this had not happened, old views would prevail and may have resulted in the permanent dissolution of Parliament.
