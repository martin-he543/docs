## [4:00 AM, Taeko Onuki](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_sOKkON_UnQ)

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/0zoGVO4bQXG8U6ChKwNgeg)

Whilst listening to a piece with a similar name by [girl in red](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zdt4UwbbQnc&ab_channel=girlinred) during an intense game of Romanian Catan with my friend, Tudor, following an interesting 70km cycle, I happened to stumble across this masterpiece having searched up the *girl in red* song after returning home.

Beginning with an atmospheric woodwind introduction, which slyly eases into a funky '70s groove as the drums enter followed by the addictive *whoosh* sound, this piece screams nostalgia. One could almost expect this piece to be playing as the background music to a Murakami novel.

44 years on, this piece feels no more dated than when it was released; in fact, I would go as far as to say it sounds rather innovative. If I were more pretentious in my writing, I would say that it is an embodiment of the optimism of the New Age Era.

The entirety of Onuki's third album, [MIGNONNE,](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rHObjo2Bu4&list=PLP1lC0FnuI0KTPg1PB1yXaRRt6NhkVRZE&ab_channel=TaekoOhnuki-Topic) is something quite different from the blaring hi-hats and snares that dominate today's music world, and is highly recommended.

## [Où Va Le Monde, La Femme](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fQDEUU1lyZQ&ab_channel=LaFemmeVEVO)

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/1JBNE7Spz0WXjXCnWSaFYl)

Funnily enough, this piece came to me recommended from my [daily French podcast](https://innerfrench.com/podcast/), which chose this piece to analyse line-by-line due to its abundance in French expressions, as well as the band being recommended by another friend of mine, Yaar.

With an unrivalled sense of bad-assery, channelling the same chord progression as [the House of the Rising Sun](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4-43lLKaqBQ&ab_channel=TheAnimalsTributeChannel), not only is the production perfect from the first vibrato of the electric guitar chord, through to the guitar solo and fade-out: once staple features of popular music, before the ADHD-age of Spotify streaming started churning out 2 minute hits, as opposed to 6 minute masterpieces.

With the brash title "Où va le monde", La Femme immediately asks the bold question of "what the hell is up with the world", which is subsequently explored throughout the song through the enchanting lyricism.

## Rosyln, Bon Iver ft. St. Vincent

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/1vsFzbhQXTqKJ3YSWxWxmJ)

Bon Iver has never made a bad piece of music, and if you disagree, fight me. From his *Blood Bank EP,* right up until his recent collaborations with Taylor Swift, and the National, Bon Iver manages to produce both a dreamy falsetto, as well as a growling bass to unmatched effect. So it might be obvious to ask, why out of an entire impressive body of work do you choose that song which featured in Twilight? Truth be told, the timbre of the guitar plays a big role in that decision.

## Satellite, Harry Styles

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/0rzaRSujxA0bKyjJl6vHYq)

*Satellite* is arguably the best song off the album, if you discount the catchiness of its lead single, the ever-ubiquitous *As It Was*, as well as the bizarreness of *Music for a Sushi Restaurant* or even *Daylight.* Perhaps the depressing ballad with the mid-piece modulation *Matlida* could qualify as well.

Regardless, Satellite makes for the ideal dreamscape of a song.

## Death with Dignity, Sufjan Stevens

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/73uJybGAhwGRD9MZYrOmTY)

Whilst its cousin, *Fourth of July* off Sufjan Stevens' album, Carrie & Lowell, which is dedicated to his parents, is more well-known, that does not mean that the other pieces of this fantastic album are to ignored either. The following makes for the ideal album opener to perhaps the most laid-back album ever created.

## Be Sweet - Korean Version, Japanese Breakfast ft. So!YoON!

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/5smkxS3MeevRwRt86AADR9)

Japanese Breakfast is truly an underrated artist. Ironically (given the band's name) for a Korean artist living in the United States, this a highly catchy, slickly-produced song. For some reason I prefer the Korean Version. Also check out their other famous song, Paprika.

## Nocturnes, Op. 48: No. 2 in F Sharp Minor. Andantino, Frédéric Chopin performed by Jan Lisiecki

[Listen on Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/track/6t8fkaoVmcsBbVPBaEwpz6)

A listen should suffice. Jan Lisiecki is a masterful musician in being an unpretentious Chopin interpretter, unlike many of his contemporaries (and myself), for maintaining a humblingly cool personality, and in this achingly beautiful Nocturne by Chopin, a version I prefer to even Rubinstein's, he executes the piece to near-perfection.
