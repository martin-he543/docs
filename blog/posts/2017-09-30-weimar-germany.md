Between 1919 and 1923, Weimar Germany faced a number of challenges. Out of these, I agree with Interpretation 2 in saying that political extremism posed the greatest challenge for the Weimar Republic.

One argument in support of Interpretation 2 is linked to the political uprisings which occurred in the early years of the Weimar Republic. For instance, Interpretation 2 suggests that the Weimar government lacked “widespread support” and was “divided about… the road ahead”. This is evidenced by uprisings such as the Sparticist Uprising in early 1919, where up to 100000 Sparticists seized government buildings and attempted to lead Germany under workers councils (soviets) and the Kapp Putsch. This posed a problem for the Weimar Republic as it became hated by both ends of the political spectrum and could satisfy neither. Consequently, this suggests that Interpretation 2 is a convincing view.

On the other hand, one argument supporting Interpretation 1 is linked to the Treaty of Versailles. For instance, Interpretation 1 suggests that Versailles was a “dictated peace”, which gained it a nickname amongst Germans of “diktat” and considered an “undeserved shame”. The Treaty of Versailles saw Germany lose 10% of its land, all its overseas colonies, much of its navy and military, accept war guilt and impossibly large reparations to pay. This posed a problem for the Weimar Republic as the loss of its pride, the military and navy, were seen as a humiliation on the world stage and the German people saw its government as back-stabbers, often referred to as the “November Criminals” for signing the armistice. Thus, this suggests that Interpretation 1 is a convincing view.

However, another argument in favour of Interpretation 2 is related to the struggles faced by Weimar Germany’s population. For instance, Interpretation 2 suggests the Weimar Government had a “bitter, struggling population” to deal with. This is accurate as Germans faced poverty-stricken conditions and were on the brink of starvation, which was exacerbated by the arrival of the Spanish flu in 1918 – 1919 which killed 750000 people. This posed a problem for the Weimar Republic as naturally they were seen as the cause of these issues and immediately after World War I they could do little to improve the plight of Germans. As such, Interpretation 2 appears to be convincing.

In conclusion, I agree with the view put forward by Interpretation 2 that political extremism and a suffering population posed the greatest challenge for the Weimar Republic. This is due to the fact that political extremism posed an existential threat to the newly-created republic and there was a good chance that many of these political uprisings could’ve been unsuccessful due to the Weimar Government’s weak grip on power.
