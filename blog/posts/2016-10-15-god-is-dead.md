I agree with the statement “The philosophies of the 19th, 20th and 21st centuries not only prove that ‘God is dead’ but that furthermore he should be”, as I believe that it is the most coherent view. One of the reasons I think this, was put forward by German Philosopher Karl Marx in his ‘Communist Manifesto’, where he argued that not only was history little more than ‘class struggles’, but also that religion was merely a method that the ruling classes (bourgeois) employed to control the working classes (proletariat.) This seems like a reasonable argument, particularly when you look at the teachings of Jesus Christ in Christianity, principally Matthew 5:3 “Blessed are the poor, for the kingdom of heaven belongs to them,” can be undoubtedly be interpreted an evil, pernicious teaching used to prevent the working classes to stage revolutionary action against their ruling overlords, by giving them claims of eternal indulgence in exchange for obedience on every level. Although my economic and political views greatly differ from that of Karl Marx’s (as I believe that not only does Communism reduce incentive to work (and thereby lower surplus and productivity), but also it suppresses the rights on an individual), I believe that his arguments concerning religion and his branding of it as the ‘opium of the people’, is effective in illustrating how religion has an adverse effect on society just like drugs (i.e. opium), it could simply be put like such: opium makes you feel good at first, but its benefits are far outweighed by its shortcomings (disorientation, anxiety, nausea, vomiting, depression, etc.), of which he argues for religion upon society. Other philosophers argue arguments supporting the above statement, one being Richard Dawkins, who argues that God cannot be the creator of the universe, and be the most intelligent being due to evolution. However, I believe that evidently his argument is flawed, in comparing supernatural beings to the natural process of evolution – a fallacy of conflation on his part. Epicurus’ argument is far more effective in proving the given question; he notices the contradiction in a God’s omnipotence and omnibenovelence, due to the existence of evil – the Problem of Evil.

However, others would disagree with this statement such as Archbishop Justin Welby or Sayyid Qutub, would argue that religion has a positive effect on society, and that God still continues to exist. Justin Welby argues that the Church has a strong role in modern society in reducing social inequality and being a force of good, through effective measures such as protecting the poor from repression in South America. Sayyid Qutub argues that rich countries (who tend to be the most secular and atheist) are unhappiest countries, of which he asserts the blame to the rise of hedonism in Western society, and the lack of religious asceticism. Furthermore, it is arguable that in trying to establish Marx’s Communist Utopia free from the ‘poison’ of religion, its impacts were bloodier than any sectarian violence brought about by religion. Notably with Stalin’s Red Terror and numerous purges in the USSR (which by comparison killed almost three times as many as Hitler’s Holocaust), Mao’s Cultural Revolution and famines in the PRC (which killed an astounding 78 million people), Pol Pot’s mass-death camps in Cambodia, all of which dwarf religious wars. As Rudolph Rummel put it, “Of all religions, secular or otherwise, Marxism is by far the bloodiest.”

I, however, disagree with the points above put forward by Justin Welby, Sayyid Qutub, and Rudolph Rummel. Firstly, the point put forward by Justin Welby can easily be refuted by the numerous controversies that have plagued the Catholic Church and many other Churches: from the treatment of Galileo, Medieval Witch Hunts, the promotion of the Crusades, the Inquisitions, blatant Anti-Semitism, their relationship with Nazi Germany, to as recently as the Rwandan Genocide during which up to a million died, which was endorsed by the Catholic Church. Religion has ultimately divided our society more than bound it together. Concerning Rudolph Rummel’s argument, the purges carried out by Marxist Dictatorships were not at all what Karl Marx intended, but rather an undesired implementation mixed with authoritarianism that led to these deaths, hence in his argument, he is committing a straw man fallacy by claim that this was what he wanted to happen.

In conclusion, my Weltanschauung overall is that religion’s existence has a negative impact on society and that ultimately God, or the God which we commonly describe of, is dead. Perhaps the solution offered by Steven J. Gould may be the answer, and that religion and science should be kept separate to their different magisteria, religion with its focus on values, and science with its focus on facts derived from empirical data.
