## Question 3
Some Salafi literalists believe that humans were created by Allah. They say that humans are a “special act of creation”, disagreeing with the notion that humans evolved from apes.
However, some Liberal Salafis think that evolution is compatible with their religious beliefs, arguing that “Allah made from water every living being” implies evolution is teleologically guided by Allah, and that Allah specifically gave humans free will and consciousness, although they are descended from apes.

## Question 4
On the one hand, some theists (such as Catholics) would argue that scientific ideas about eh universe’s creation are indeed compatible with religion. Catholics, for instance, would suggest that God set of the chain of events that caused the Big Bang, and that the 7 days of creation, when interpreted figuratively reflect the Big Bang Theory. For instance, the Gospel of John paints a more figurative interpretation: “In the beginning was the word and the word was God…”
	On the other hand, other theists (such as Young Earth Creationists) would completely disagree with scientific ideas. They would argue that evidence for the Big Bang are simply tricks created by the devil (e.g. the Earth appears to look old).

## Question 5
Members of the Catholic Church such as Pope John Paul II and Liberal Salafis would disagree with this statement, arguing that Evolution by Natural Selection, rather than disproving religious ideas actually strengthen them with God teleologically guiding evolution. They would cite religious texts such as the Qu’ran – “Allah made from water every living being” pointing to the primordial soup where life began; or they would suggest hermeneutically interpreting Genesis in a non-literal way.
	On the other hand, atheists such as Richard Dawkins would agree with this point of view, arguing that the universe arising from chance and not a deity is the simplest explanation for the existence of the universe (by Ockham’s razor). They would suggest design that appears to be intelligent is the product of billions of years of evolution, rather than the work of a God.
	However, Steven J. Gould would suggest that a NOMA (non-overlapping magisteria) approach should be employed. He would argue that religion and theology should stick to its domain of higher truths and morality derived from mysticism whilst science should stick to facts derived from empirical data. As suggested by Galilleo, “Science shows us how the heavens go, religion shows us how to go to heaven”.
	I disagree with the statement above that evolution disproves religious ideas pertaining to the origins of the universe. Firstly, a teleologically-guided model of evolution is plausible, and secondly, I disagree with Gould who suggests that religion and science can be separated as I believe that religion inevitably intrudes on science’s turf and vice versa. Although I am not a theist, a believe that evolution alone does not provide sufficient evidence to disprove religious ideas on the origins of the universe.
