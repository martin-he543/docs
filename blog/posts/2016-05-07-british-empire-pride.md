The British Empire was a vast territorial network of countries throughout the British Empire, so vast in fact that the famous saying ‘The Sun Never sets in the British Empire’ has come to be. The Empire reached its territorial peak during the late nineteenth century until its decline in the twentieth. Some argue that its actions undermine what Britain stands for and should be forgotten whereas others say we should be proud of how it shaped out world into one of democracy and freedom. I, however, believe that the empire was a sin we should not be proud of.

Some people argue that the British Empire was an achievement that we should be proud of. They think this because it helped spread British ideas around the land occupied by Britain, and the impact of this I still seen all around the world today. As put by Naill Ferguson, “if you like the Modern World then you can’t ignore its debt to the British empire.” It helped to spread ideas of freedom and liberty around the world, as well as protecting common people with the law. It unified people under one language – English, as well as one religion – Protestantism, and goods that could not be manufactured in countries were exported from Britain. Infrastructure such as roads and trains were introduced in these foreign countries as well as a wide range of sports. Britain also benefited greatly. Ports such Bristol and Liverpool became very wealthy, the empire has influenced our culture in terms of language and food, and the empire enabled poor people (such as Scottish highlanders in the 19th century) were able to start new lives in parts of the empire. Thus, the empire was something we should be proud of.

However, in achieving this, the Empire committed many unjust acts. Those that rebelled under the British suffered greatly. Britain created theories of racial supremacy (not unlike the Nazis) to justify their role in other countries, leaving a legacy of racism that can still be seen today, and was seen in South Africa as apartheid, a form of racial segregation. The first concentration camps where 30000 people died were also set up by the British. Top jobs in the colonies were taken by the British, natives weren’t given a chance. Native populations of colonies suffered hard under the rule of the British; the Aboriginal population fell by 90% and those that looked for a new life in the settler’s cities found themselves discriminated against and suffered alcoholism. Diseases brought by the British massacred Native Americans and Aboriginal people. 11 million African people were displaced through immense cruelty that has left a legacy of ‘rebellion, repression and resistance’ as said by Richard Gott. In India frequent famines led to the deaths of up to 25 million people, and a million people died in a brutal civil war after Britain left India in a divided community of Hindus and Muslims. Therefore, the British empire is something we should not be proud about.

In conclusion, there are some aspects of the British empire we can be proud of, however the bad things the empire did far outweigh its good points. The number of people it badly treated is comparable to the actions of the Adolf Hitler and Stalin in modern day terms. Although it helped spread British values, it has left the modern world with many problems we still haven’t sorted out such as racism, poverty and famine.
