I think that Sir Douglas Haig was the butcher, but not a bungler of the Somme. First of all, to be a butcher, one has to brutally murder many people – I think that such a word is perfect to describe the actions of Sir Douglas Haig. He willingly sent onslaughts upon onslaughts of men to their premature deaths, and stuck by his tactics with complete disregard of the huge losses of lives that occurred due to such terrible decisions. On the first day of the Somme, some 40000 British casualties were sustained. Had Haig been one to embrace technology, and embraced technologies such as tanks, aeroplanes, and even machine guns, then such a magnificent slaughter of men could have been averted. It is the immortalised quotes of his, such as “the way to capture machine guns is through grit and determination” or “aeroplanes and tanks are only accessories to men” that help portray him and solidify the portrayal that he is an outdated military commander that is completely out of touch with his soldiers. While this is true, I believe that he was not the bungler of the Somme. While he only made an advancement of 7 miles throughout the five-month campaign, he succeeded in his original aim of taking off pressure at Verdun where the situation was so grim that members of the French army were mutinying. The artillery bombardment and attack at the Somme forced the Germans to divert some of their army from Verdun to the Somme to ease pressure on the French army, and the casualties from the German army far surpassed the ones that the British suffered, so much so that the army was rendered into a state of disarray that made the German advance have to retreat 15 miles. Also, the German military commander Paul von Hindenburg was replaced by Falkenhayn as a result of the Somme. Therefore, I believe that Haig was not the Bungler of the Somme as although one could view as despite the great loss of life, in retrospect, his actions helped to secure the Allied Victory of WWI.
