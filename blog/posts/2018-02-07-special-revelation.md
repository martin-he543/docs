## Question 4
A secular view would be that design does not require a god spearheading it, but rather design is merely a matter of chance. They would argue that design that appears to be intelligent around us actually comes about as the product of billions of years of evolution from natural selection.

On the other hand, a theist such as Ibn Rushd might argue that design is proof of the existence of a deity. They would suggest that such complexities present in nature, such as the Fibonacci sequence found in the ratios of the human body, could not be a product of chance, but rather is the result of a god. These ideas resonate in Genesis: “through him all things were made; without him nothing was made that has been made”.

## Question 5
A Sufi such as Al-Ghazzali would disagree with this statement. They would argue that special revelation is the primary source of knowledge, and that special revelation helps understands of intellectually-difficult part of the Qu’ran. They would take the example of Muhammed who at first believed he was sick, but after 113 further revelations realised he wasn’t mistaken. Christian mystics such as Dionysius would argue that in order to understand God, we first have to shed empirical and rationalist knowledge before achieving henosis and theosis (mystical union with God), and that attempting to gain an empirco-rational understanding of a supernatural God is impossible.

However, some people like David Hume would agree with this statement, stating “extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.” In addition to this, they would put into question the credibility of the sources that undermine the legitimacy of special revelation. Finally, he would say that mystical experiences from one religion would cancel out all other special revelation in other religions were they to be true. Finally, Karl Marx would cite that mystical experiences were part of the “act” created by the bourgeois to manipulate the working classes and thus those who receive special revelation are mistaken.

On the contrary, philosophers like William James would argue against Hume’s argument saying that if belief leads to positive consequences to one’s life, then we may reasonably believe it. Also one could argue that “extraordinary evidence” required to back up claims could be lost during the purification stage where one loses a sense of rationalism. It could even be argued that special revelation is ineffable. So, cannot be articulated, but can be “felt”.

In conclusion, I disagree with the statement. For one, special revelation is ineffable, and should not be reasoned, thus it is impossible to ascertain whether or not those that receive it are mistaken. However, I would say that special revelation isn’t a sufficient by a necessary condition for understanding God, and that by employing a duplex modus approach and using both special revelation and general revelation, would deliver the most fulfilling option, where they are necessary but sufficient together.
