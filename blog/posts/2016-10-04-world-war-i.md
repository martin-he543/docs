World War One was the most consequential event of the twentieth century. It brought colossal empires ruled by monarchies tumbling to the ground, wiping off centuries, if not millennia of tradition. Its impacts were so great, that people thought it would be the war to end all wars (how wrong they could be). It was the Great War, a war that created a lost generation, and ushered in the modern age. Despite this, what directly caused World War One is still widely disputed. However, I believe that ultimately it was alliances that caused and propagated World War One to a much larger scale as they not only gave rise to militarism, but helped to escalate much smaller conflicts to ones ultimately between the great power blocks of Europe and their allies.

On the one hand, one could attribute World War One to the Assassination of the Archduke Franz Ferdinand. They would argue this, as ultimately, it was the events of the 28th of June, that led to World War One. The assassination not only allowed the Austro-Hungarian Empire to direct their anger towards Serbia (whom had been backed by Russia), and an excuse to deliver an incredibly unreasonable ultimatum (which proposed but inevitably led to a sever of diplomatic ties between Austria and Serbia, mobilisation and threats, that dragged the whole of Europe into war.) Therefore, one could argue that the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand was the trigger of World War One. Had the Archduke not been assassinated, then such a ‘mobilisation’ crisis could have been averted, that brought in all the main power blocks and affiliated countries into the First World War, and the alliances simply would’ve continued to exist. Thus, the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand did help to cause World War One.

Others, including me, argue that it was the alliances that dragged Europe into the First World War. This is because, the alliances helped to bring Europe to a point of tension, that any event could’ve set the spark for World War I. The alliances were supposed to make two equally-matched blocks of power which promised to aid each other militarily, but inevitably, if one block was more powerful than the other, it would encourage competition between the two accumulating in rapid militarism (as did happen.) This militarism not only increase tension, but also gave individual countries confidence that to wage wars (and the alliances provided further confidence as other countries would be obliged to stand in and help.) Furthermore, the geographical advantage of the Triple Entente over the Triple Alliance put Germany in the awkward situation of having to fight a war on two fronts (i.e. Schlieffen Plan), helped to further raise tensions. Therefore, the alliances helped to cause World War One.

In conclusion, I believe that it was the alliances that brought Europe to war rather than Franz Ferdinand’s assassination, as without the alliances, it would’ve been very unlikely that the war would’ve been elevated to world stage, and it probably would’ve remained a regional conflict. In other words, although Franz Ferdinand’s assassination was the direct cause of World War I, it wouldn’t have sparked a world war if it hadn’t been for the alliances and the mix of problems facing pre-1914 Europe, such as the Balkan’s crisis, the rise of militarism and imperialism as well as growing nationalism that meant Europe embraced war, rather than feared it.
